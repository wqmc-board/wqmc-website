Seventy-one flats, one set of neighbours.

Welland Quarter is Swift House and Soar House, on the River Welland a few minutes from the town centre. The blocks are run by the people who live in them, through a management company the leaseholders own.

The development

Run by neighbours, not landlords.

The two blocks went up on a former industrial plot beside the river, with the first leases granted in December 2009 on 150-year terms (lease details from a Soar House sale). Owner-occupiers, families and tenants live side by side across the 71 flats.

Swift House and Soar House share their grounds, bin and cycle stores, parking, the basement plant and the services that keep the lights on — so they are budgeted and maintained as one development rather than two.

Welland Quarter Management Company Ltd is owned by the leaseholders. Its directors are neighbours who do the job unpaid: they appoint the managing agent, set the service charge budget and hold the compliance programme to its dates. Letters Block Management handles the day-to-day work under an agreement the board reviews every year.

Two things make that work: people telling the board what they see, and people reporting faults the moment they appear. Both have a route on this site.

Compliance first

Fire risk assessments, electrical installation condition reports, lightning protection, water hygiene, lifts and emergency lighting all run to a dated compliance calendar rather than being picked up as they fall due.

One address for the board

Every director reads directors@wqmc.org. Writing there means your point reaches the whole board and is on the record for the next meeting.

Planned, not reactive

Works are scheduled against a planned maintenance programme and published on the notice board before they start, so residents know what is happening and when.

Contact

Who to speak to, and in what order.

Almost everything starts with the managing agent. The board handles the things only the board can decide.
